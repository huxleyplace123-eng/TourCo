Be honest about the gain column in Phase 1. These are modelled estimates, and they must be labelled as such in the product. Real measured lift — "median +6.2 points, three-week lag, n=41" — only exists once the intervention log has run against a cohort for a few months.

Which is exactly why the interventions table ships in week one, months before anything can be computed from it. The data is only ever valuable in hindsight, and you cannot backfill it.

Scope

What's in, and what's deliberately not.

Phase 1 covers all six surfaces, but at presence-and-integrity depth rather than full rank tracking. That distinction is what holds the build to five weeks and the running cost to near zero.

Ships in Phase 1

  • Owned property audit — render mode, schema, Core Web Vitals, content depth, and the AI-crawler block check
  • Belief audit — 5 models × 12 questions × 10 runs, diffed against the NMLS record, with confidence intervals
  • Google Business Profile — 14 completeness fields, categories, photos, Q&A, review response rate
  • Review velocity — Google, Zillow, Experience.com, BBB. Recency-weighted, not raw count
  • NAP consistency across 41 directories, plus mortgage-vertical presence
  • Brand defense — who is bidding on their name, via one SERP call per scan
  • Social presence across 9 platforms at profile level
  • Company and officer scoring — the same six surfaces applied to a mortgage lender, a mortgage brokerage, or one loan officer
  • 3-competitor comparison on identical checks, ranked in one cohort table
  • Intervention logging — no user-facing output yet, but recording from day one

Deliberately deferred

  • Full 120-prompt persona panel — Phase 2. The 12-question belief set is enough to sell
  • Geo-gridded map rank — expensive per scan, and the free tier can't carry it
  • Google organic rank tracking — the single largest cost line. Add it when customers pay
  • Citation graph — needs cohort-scale data before it means anything
  • Measured lift attribution — needs months of intervention history
  • Branch-tier rollups — mortgage loan officer and company scoring both ship; the middle tier can wait
  • OAuth social depth — requires an account connection flow, which breaks the no-signup promise
  • The public Index — build the engine first, publish the rankings later
Build

Five weeks, in order of what unblocks what.

Each week ends with something demonstrable. The sequence is deliberate: the free deterministic collectors come first so there is a working report before a single paid API is wired up.

Week 1 Spine and crawler Schema, job queue, immutable evidence store, site crawler, AI-crawler block check. Ends with a scored owned-property report.
Week 2 Ground truth and local NMLS matching, Google Business Profile, NAP sweep, review velocity. Ends with four of six surfaces live and still $0 in API spend.
Week 3 Belief audit Five-model runner, N-run statistics, extraction and truth-diffing. The centrepiece. Ends with the first real hallucination caught on a live lender.
Week 4 Scoring and competitors Versioned weight config, cohort ranking, the fix engine. Ends with the report screen you just used.
Week 5 Public surface Scan page, streaming progress, shareable report URLs, PDF export, intervention logging. Ends live and taking traffic.

Why the free tier survives.

The architectural decision that makes this work: score by query, not by customer. One run of "best FHA lender in Tampa" scores every Tampa lender named in the answer, simultaneously. Build the pipeline query-first now and the marginal cost of the eleventh lender in a market is close to zero. Build it customer-first and you will rewrite the whole collection layer somewhere around customer thirty.

To start

Six decisions, then I build.

  1. A repository. Not TourCo — that's the Costa Rica tourism app. This needs its own home, and I can scaffold it.
  2. Twenty seed lenders. Real ones, one metro. Name the market and I'll assemble the list.
  3. NMLS access method. Scrape Consumer Access or license a data feed. This sets the ceiling on belief-audit accuracy.
  4. Per-officer scoring — in or out? Answer before week 3. It changes the entity model and retrofitting it is expensive.
  5. A monthly API ceiling. Sets run count, model tier, and how many free scans you can absorb.
  6. A name. TrueNorth and "Answer Share" are placeholders I picked to have something to draw with.

My recommendation on sequencing: build weeks 1–3 before showing anyone. The owned-property audit alone is a working lead magnet, but the belief audit is what makes a lender forward the report to their CEO. Launching with the first and without the second spends your one good first impression on a commodity.
